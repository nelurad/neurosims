NeuroSims

Interactive simulations for visualizing neuroscience concepts

Each simulation runs in the browser, with nothing to install, and can be embedded directly into Canvas, Moodle, or any learning management system. They were built for Introduction to Neuroscience (PSB 2000) at Florida State University, and any teacher or student is welcome to use them.

Cells of the Nervous System

The building blocks: what neurons look like, how their shapes differ, and how a signal travels through each one. A good place to start.

Types of Neurons

Compare multipolar, bipolar, pseudounipolar and unipolar neurons: count the processes leaving the cell body and watch which way a signal travels through each.

Studying the Brain

The tools neuroscientists use to see the brain's structure and activity, change it, and look at its cells.

Seeing the Brain

Compare CT, MRI, DTI, fMRI, PET, fNIRS, EEG/MEG and TMS: slice through a brain, watch the slow BOLD response, see Alzheimer’s on PET and drag a TMS coil over the cortex. Then stain tissue with Nissl, Golgi, c-fos, tract tracers and Brainbow, and pick the right tool for each research question.

Neurophysiology

How a single neuron fires, and how it passes the message to the next one. Start with the action potential, then move to the synapse.

The Salty Banana

A step-by-step story of the action potential: resting potential, threshold, spike, refractory period, and myelin.

The Action Potential Graph

Click anywhere on the action potential curve to zoom in on the membrane and see which sodium and potassium channels are open at that moment.

Saltatory Conduction

Why the action potential seems to "jump" from node to node along a myelinated axon, and what happens when myelin is damaged.

Exploring Synapses

Release a neurotransmitter, watch it cross the gap, and see how receptors and drugs change the receiving cell.

Neurotransmitter Pathways

Pick a neurotransmitter and watch where it starts in the brain, where its fibers travel, what they do, and what happens when they go wrong.

Dopamine: Movement vs. Motivation

Trace dopamine's two big pathways, one for movement and one for reward, and see what happens when each goes wrong.

Clearing the Synapse

See the three ways a synapse gets cleaned up after a signal: reuptake back into the neuron, enzymes breaking the transmitter apart, and diffusion away from the gap. Then see what happens when a drug blocks one of them.

Receptor Mechanisms

See how molecules fit into receptors like keys in a lock. Some switch the receptor on (agonists), and others fit but block it (antagonists). Find out why that's how so many drugs work.

Sensation and Perception

How signals from your senses reach the brain and become what you feel, see, hear, taste and smell, from receptors to relay stations to cortex.

Sensation in the Brain

Stimulate the touch and movement strips beside the central sulcus, meet the homunculus, and follow a signal from your hand to its own patch of cortex.

Signal Relay

A game: route touch, pain, sight, sound, taste and smell to the right part of the brain, and catch the exceptions. Smell skips the thalamus, and a hot stove triggers a reflex first.

From Light to Sight

Follow vision stage by stage: focusing, phototransduction, center-surround cells, the optic chiasm, line-detecting neurons in V1, and the what and where streams.

Sleep and Brain Rhythms

Color and dreams, the body's master clock, the stages of sleep, and the brain waves that tie them together.

From Sight to Sleep

A follow-along lab: find your blind spot, mix cone colors, run a hamster running-wheel experiment, read a night of sleep EEG, sync a crowd of neurons, then download your worksheet.

The Sleeping Brain

Brain waves from scratch, an animated journey through one night, what EEG and ERPs measure, brain waves of each sleep stage, a night of sleep cycles, how blue light and melanopsin set the body clock, why you get sleepy, the brain's sleep-wake switch, what sleep is for, sleep deprivation, and sleep disorders. Has a Present mode for projecting in class.

Emotion and Stress

Where feelings come from: the body's autonomic wiring, theories of emotion, and the brain circuits for reward, fear, aggression and stress.

The Emotional Brain

Switch between fight-or-flight and rest-and-digest, animate James–Lange vs. Cannon–Bard vs. Schachter, test the facial feedback hypothesis with a pen and Botox, place a reward electrode, lesion the limbic system, race the amygdala's low and high roads, and trigger the HPA stress axis. Has a Present mode for projecting in class.

Learning and Memory

The brain systems that store different kinds of memories, and the synaptic changes that make learning possible.

The Remembering Brain

Patient H.M. and the medial temporal lobe, mirror tracing, an interactive map of memory systems, the stages from sensory buffer to long-term memory, consolidation, false memories, emotional memory, and place and grid cells.

The Learning Brain

Habituation in Aplysia, Pavlov’s dog, eyeblink conditioning in the cerebellum, a Skinner box, synaptic changes, enriched environments and dendrites, Hebb’s rule, LTP in the hippocampus, and how NMDA and AMPA receptors detect coincidences.

Developmental Neuroscience

How the brain builds and refines its wiring from birth through adolescence.

Synaptic Pruning

Watch a young brain overproduce synapses, then keep the ones experience uses and remove the rest.

Exam Review

Interactive walk-throughs of the ideas students most often mix up, with a picture for each one.

Common Mix-Ups: Exams 1 and 2

Sixteen tricky topics, each shown as the mix-up next to what's actually true, with something to click, drag or play. Covers afferent vs. efferent, brain anatomy, research methods, resting potential, refractory periods, receptors, transmitter families, dopamine pathways, dose-response curves, tolerance and alcohol.
